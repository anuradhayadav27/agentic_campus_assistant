🎙️ The Perfect 1-Minute Project Pitch (Ruben, Anupa, Erick)

"Good morning, professors. Our project for CSE276 is an Agentic AI Assistant for Multi-Step Campus Service Workflows. Unlike traditional chatbots that simply match static keywords and answer one question at a time, our system implements an autonomous ReAct (Reasoning + Acting) agent loop. When a student gives a complex goal like 'Check my CSE276 attendance, book Lab 34-601 tomorrow, and report a broken projector', our agent:
1. Decomposes the goal into atomic subtasks,
2. Selects and invokes tools across university departments,
3. Observes execution feedback and dynamically re-plans if a slot is full or a book is out of stock, and
4. Safely escalates to human authorities when administrative intervention is needed.
Our benchmark shows a 93.8% multi-step success rate compared to only 36.4% for traditional rule-based chatbots."

❓ Top Viva Questions & Answers

Q1: What makes this system 'Agentic' instead of just a regular chatbot?

Answer: A traditional chatbot is passive and single-turn (input query → text output). An Agentic AI assistant has agency: it can formulate a plan, decide which tools to call in what order, inspect the results of each action, autonomously adapt or re-plan when an action fails (e.g. finding alternative lab slots), and persist state across multi-step campus workflows.

Q2: What is Goal Decomposition and why is it essential?

Answer: Students frequently express compound needs in natural language (e.g., "Check attendance and book lab"). Goal decomposition is the algorithmic process of parsing a compound goal into an ordered sequence of discrete subgoals $[g_1, g_2, \dots, g_n]$, each mapped to an executable function with typed parameters. Without decomposition, rule-based systems drop all but the first intent.

Q3: How does your Dynamic Re-Planning mechanism work?

Answer: When a tool execution returns a conflict—such as SLOT_FULL_CONFLICT when booking a lab or COPIES_EXHAUSTED at the library—the agent intercepts the failure state instead of crashing. It evaluates the tool's suggestions, selects the nearest available slot or related book in the same category, and executes a compensatory tool call, logging the reasoning trace for complete accountability.

Q4: How did you implement Human Escalation?

Answer: The agent has dedicated guardrails that identify out-of-domain requests, administrative grievances, disciplinary appeals, or repeated execution failures. It invokes the escalate_to_human_admin tool, creating an official tracking ticket for the Dean of Student Affairs / Grievance Cell, ensuring sensitive institutional policies are protected.

Q5: How did you compare against the Rule-Based Baseline?

Answer: We implemented a scripted regex and finite-state chatbot as a baseline and evaluated both systems on 6 standard campus edge cases. The rule-based system suffered from single-intent bottlenecks (dropping 63.6% of secondary tasks) and zero error recovery, whereas our Agentic AI achieved 93.8% multi-step task completion and 100% successful re-planning.
